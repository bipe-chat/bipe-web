# Bipe's positions
_Updated 18 November 2026._

Bipe is a messaging app. It has no party and asks no one for a vote. When a law changes the rules of the market Bipe works in, Bipe says what it thinks and why. The positions are on this page.

## Bill PL 4.675/2025, on digital markets

### What the bill does
The bill changes Brazil's competition law. It lets Cade, the body that already oversees competition in Brazil, set rules for the largest digital companies. Only groups with more than R$ 5 billion a year in revenue in Brazil, or more than R$ 50 billion worldwide, are in scope. Even then, Cade looks at each case and explains each decision, and the company has the right to defend itself.

Some of the possible rules: install apps from outside the official store, take your data to another service, choose another app as the default, and talk across different services.

### What Bipe thinks
Bipe supports the bill. It gives new apps, from Brazil or abroad, a fair chance to reach people. And it gives each person more choice over the apps they use.

### What Bipe asks for with it
Talking across different apps is only worth it if it is safe. Bipe asks for four conditions:
- End-to-end encryption stays on both sides. No one in between reads the conversation, not the companies and not the government.
- The apps connect through an open, published standard that any app can follow.
- Each person chooses whether to talk with people on another app. Nothing turns on by itself.
- No one scans message content or traces who sent what.

## Questions about this position

### Is supporting the bill taking a side?
No. Bipe supports the bill because it opens the market to new apps. The European Union passed similar rules in 2022, with votes from parties on every side.

### Is it against foreign companies?
No. The rule goes by the size of the company, whatever its home country. A Brazilian company above the limit follows the same rules. Bipe wants Brazilian apps to be able to exist and grow next to the ones that already exist.

### Does Bipe want a rule against some app?
No. The rules are the same for every large company. Bipe also asks for no exception for itself.

### And if Bipe grows?
If Bipe ever passes the limit, it follows the same rules.

### Does opening conversations across apps make everything less safe?
It can, if it is done badly. That is why the four conditions above exist. Security and privacy come first. Within those limits, a company that says opening up brings a risk must show the risk in technical terms.

### Does the bill allow taking an app offline?
Bipe thinks a law needs consequences for those who do not comply. Suspending a service should be the last step: only in serious and repeated cases, after a notice and a deadline to fix it, and always protecting the people who use the service.

### Does Bipe let you take your data with you?
Yes. In the app, Download my data gives you everything Bipe keeps about you. Messages are not in that file, because Bipe cannot read them: they are end-to-end encrypted.

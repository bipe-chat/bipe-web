# Privacy policy
_Bipe, test version. Updated 9 November 2026._

Bipe is a Brazilian messaging app, still in testing. This page tells you plainly what data we keep, where it lives and why. The Portuguese version is the reference text.

## Who looks after your data
During testing, Bipe is run by Envisioning. Questions or requests about your data: [Contact us](/en/help/).

## What we keep
- **Your account:** username, display name, the connected devices and protected forms of your PIN, of the access the app creates and of the code to get back into your account. We never see the PIN itself. Accounts from the old test also sign in with an email and a password they chose, kept only in protected form.
- **Your email (optional):** used to get you back into your account if you lose your phone, and to tell you when something changes in it, such as a new PIN, a new device or a request to delete the account. Locaweb delivers the emails. We don't send marketing and never show your email to anyone in the app.
- **Waitlist:** if you leave your email on the site, we store the email, the platform (Android or iPhone), the language and the site page where you signed up. When your turn comes, we send one email with an invite that works for 14 days. We delete your address when the invite is used or expires, or sooner if you ask. It is used for nothing else.
- **Organisation request:** if an organisation uses the form on the page for organisations, we store the organisation's name, kind and CNPJ, the name, role and email of the person who wrote, how many customers it serves, the channels it uses, what it pays today to talk to customers, what it wants, what it would use Bipe for, its biggest problem when it talks to customers, when it wants to start, the page of the website it came from, the message and the language. We keep this data in Starlings, Envisioning's work system, on servers in the European Union. The data of people who use the app stays in Brazil. It is used only to answer and to learn what organisations pay today, and it is deleted after one year, or sooner if you ask.
- **Your messages:** private conversations are end-to-end encrypted. The server stores them scrambled and can't read them. In rooms without encryption, the server stores the text. Who can read what, in plain words: [Who can read your chats](/en/who-can-read/).
- **Connection data:** each time you connect, we log the IP address, source port, date and time. These logs stay for 6 months, encrypted after the first hour, as Brazil's Marco Civil da Internet (article 15) asks, and then we delete them. The server also keeps, per account and device, the IP addresses used and the last time of each, for up to 6 months. The last access of each device stays while the device is connected.
- **Photos and files:** what you send stays on the server so the people you send it to can download it.
- **Phone number and contact lookup:** sign-up does not ask for your number. If you let the app read your contacts, it sends the address-book numbers to the Bipe server over an encrypted connection. Names stay on the phone. The server compares the numbers with the numbers linked to accounts and does not keep the list. Linking a number to your account is optional: today only the Bipe team does it, when you ask. Being found by your number starts off, and only you turn it on. A number nobody confirmed for 12 months leaves the lookup and is deleted 30 days later.
- **Verification:** if you verify your identity with gov.br, the server keeps the verified name and a protected version of the CPF. The signed document is not stored. If a work account asks for the company seal, the server keeps the CNPJ, the proof and the company's public data at Receita Federal and the CVM.
- **Calls:** to carry audio and video, Bipe's call server sees your IP, your account, the chat and when you join and leave. The call content is end-to-end encrypted.
- **Reports:** when you report a message, the team gets the message you chose to send, the chat and the reason.
- **AI assistants:** if you add an AI assistant to a chat, the messages in that chat reach whoever runs the assistant. The app shows first whether it runs in Brazil, outside or in an unknown place.
- **Chat with Bipe Support and problem reports:** the text goes to Starlings, the team's work system, in the European Union. Photos, files, technical logs and screenshots stay on the Bipe server only.
- **Message from the website:** if you write through the form on the help page, we store your name, your email, the message, the language and the replies in the conversation. We keep this data in Starlings, Envisioning's work system, on servers in the European Union. An email carries a private link so you can continue the conversation. The data stays until you ask us to delete it.
- **Milestones:** the app sends no statistics. From what the server already sees, it notes a few milestones per day: account created, first sign-in, first message, contact verified, invite sent or used, group created, call started, fifth contact and day of use, plus the platform (iPhone, Android or web). Each one is tied to a code derived from your account, not to your username, with no content and nothing about who you talk to. It tells us whether Bipe is useful and at which step people give up. It is kept for 13 months.
- **Clipboard:** when the app opens without an account, it asks the phone whether something is copied, without reading it. If there is, the app shows a "Paste" button. It reads what you copied only when you tap that button, and uses only the invite code it finds. That code goes to the Bipe server, the same as when you open an invite link. On Android installed from Google Play, the app also reads the invite that the store passes along with the install.
- **Age range:** Bipe does not ask for your date of birth. The app asks the phone which age range you are in. On iPhone, Apple answers, and only if you agree to share. On Android installed from Google Play, Google answers. The server keeps only the range (under 13, 13 to 15, 16 to 17, or 18 and over), where it came from and the date. When the phone gives no answer, the account has no range. The range gives accounts of people under 18 the protections that Brazil's ECA Digital requires, and an account with no range gets the same protections. Nobody in the app sees your range. It is kept as long as the account exists.

## What we don't do
- No ads.
- We don't sell or pass your data to advertisers.
- No third-party analytics or crash reporting. The app sends no usage statistics.
- On the site, we count visits with Umami, a statistics program that runs on Bipe's own server in Brazil. It uses no cookies, keeps no IP address and does not know who you are. It keeps only the page, the site you came from, the device and browser type, the language and the approximate city, for 13 months. Invite codes and usernames are removed from the address before it reaches the server. If your browser asks not to be tracked, nothing is counted.
- On the site we also test which home page title works best: your browser keeps which version you saw, and the server only adds up visits and clicks per version, without knowing who visited.
- We don't ask for your phone number to create the account.

## Where your data lives
In Brazil. The servers are in São Paulo, with a Brazilian cloud company, and the encrypted backups are in Brazil too. Bipe doesn't exchange messages with other servers: federation is off. What leaves Brazil:

- **Starlings, in the European Union:** the organisation request, the message from the website, and the text of the chat with Bipe Support and of problem reports.
- **Apple:** notification alerts on iPhone, the proof at sign-up that the app is Bipe, the code to get back into your account in your iCloud Keychain, your age range if you agree, and your Apple account if you link it.
- **Google:** notification alerts on Android installed from Google Play, the code to get back into your account in your Google account's Block Store, your age range, the invite Google Play hands over with the install, and your Google account if you link it.
- **Whoever runs an AI assistant**, if you add one to a chat.
- **Locaweb**, which delivers Bipe's emails. The country of its server is not yet confirmed.

## Notifications
Once notifications are on, the notification service of Apple (iPhone) or Google (Android) gets only an alert that a message arrived: codes for the chat and the message, the unread count and a random address for the account. Never the content or who sent it.

## Your rights (LGPD)
You can ask to see, correct or delete your data and your account through the contact above. In the Bipe app, you delete your account in You > Your account > Delete account. It is deleted 72 hours after the request, and until then you can cancel on any of your devices. In the old app, Bipe Chat, accounts are deleted on request through the contact above. After the account is deleted, only the date, a protected version of the CPF and the seal record stay, for 365 days, so nobody gets around a block with a new account. Every piece of data, with its legal basis and retention, is in [Your data on Bipe](/en/lgpd/).

## How long
Most of it, as long as your account exists. Some things have their own limit: connection logs stay 6 months, as the law requires; usage milestones and site visits, 13 months; problem reports, 90 days; an invite nobody used, 90 days after it expires. Backups keep everything for up to 37 days, so data you deleted leaves them when they expire. If we restore a copy, we delete again what was deleted after it. Some data has no limit set yet, such as reports. Every limit is in [Your data on Bipe](/en/lgpd/). When testing ends, we may delete test accounts and messages, always with notice first.
